    How to use the gift budget calculator

    1. Enter your total budget: everything you plan to spend on gifts this season, in one number.
    2. Choose how much to keep for extras. Wrapping, cards and postage come out of the same pocket, so set them aside first.
    3. Add each person with a group and a priority. Higher priority means a bigger share of what is left.
    4. Already know a price? Type it into Set amount. That person keeps it, and the rest is shared among everyone else.
    5. As you shop, enter what you paid in Spent. The totals show what is left and turn red if you go over.

    Your list saves itself in this browser. To keep a copy elsewhere or share it with a partner, use Export CSV (opens in any spreadsheet) or Print list for a tidy checklist to take shopping.

    Gift budgeting tips that actually help

    Budget from cash, not hope

    Set the total at what you can pay off by the end of January, from savings or the next couple of paychecks. If last year's card statement disagrees with your memory, trust the statement. Most people underestimate because they count the gifts and forget everything around them.

    Keep 10 to 15% for the extras

    Wrapping paper, tape, cards, postage, batteries and the neighbor who surprises you with cookies add up fast. That is what the extras reserve is for. Whatever is left in December is a head start on next year.

    Decide the total first, then divide

    Working top-down stops the slow creep of "just a little more" on every person. When one gift runs over, take the difference from someone else's line or from the extras, rather than letting the total drift.

    Suggest a limit early

    Families and offices are often relieved when someone proposes a spending cap, or a gift exchange where each person buys for one drawn name. Raise it in October or early November, before anyone has started shopping.

    For kids: want, need, wear, read

    One thing they want, one they need, one to wear and one to read. It keeps things fair between siblings and puts a natural ceiling on the pile.

    Log it the moment you buy

    Type what you paid into Spent straight away, and keep receipts together, with gift receipts for anything that might be exchanged. Note return windows: some stores extend them for the holidays, many do not.

    A sale only saves money if it was on the list

    A discounted item nobody needed is spending, not saving. Watch shipping cut-off dates too, because paying for express delivery in the last week quietly cancels out the discounts you found.

    Start next year in January

    Divide this year's total by twelve and set that aside each month. Next December becomes a withdrawal instead of a bill.
